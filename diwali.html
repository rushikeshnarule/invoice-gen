Sacred Traditions

The Five Days of Diwali

Each day of Deepavali carries profound spiritual significance, honoring prosperity, the victory of righteousness, familial love, and gratitude.

Day 1 • Dhanteras

Dhanatrayodashi

Worship of Dhanvantari & Prosperity

Dedicated to the physician of the gods, Lord Dhanvantari, for good health, and Goddess Lakshmi for prosperity. Homes are cleaned and auspicious metals purchased.

Day 2 • Naraka Chaturdashi

Choti Diwali

Triumph of Light over Darkness

Commemorates the vanquishing of the demon Narakasura by Lord Krishna and Satyabhama. Celebrated with the morning Abhyanga Snan bath and lighting of diyas.

Day 3 • Main Diwali

Lakshmi Pujan

Deepavali & The New Year

The pinnacle of the festival. Families gather for Lakshmi-Ganesh Puja, illuminate homes with rows of glowing clay diyas, burst fireworks, and share sweets.

Day 4 • Govardhan Puja

Annakut & Padwa

Gratitude to Nature & Devotion

Honors Lord Krishna lifting the Govardhan mountain to shelter villagers from torrential rains. Huge offerings of vegetarian delicacies are prepared in gratitude.

Day 5 • Bhai Dooj

Bhratri Dwitiya

Sacred Bond of Siblings

Sisters apply a holy tilak on their brothers' foreheads and pray for their long life and well-being, while brothers bestow gifts and vows of enduring protection.
